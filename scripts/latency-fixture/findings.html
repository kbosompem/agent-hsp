1. Preachers rarely say "John 3:16". Across these sermons they gave 99 full references. They also opened a chapter and then said "verse 6 says" 36 times, and quoted scripture without naming it 119 times. The app was built for the first habit only.
  2. Full references caught rose from 67% to 82% (66 to 81 of 99) after this round of parser fixes. Wrong verses fell from 31 to 27.
  3. Follow-ups now work. "Turn to Luke 8 … verse 18" used to show nothing. With the app now tracking the open chapter, it catches 21 of 36 of these, up from 0.
  4. Most remaining misses are mishearings by the speech model, not parser gaps. Examples: Hosea heard as "Isaiah", Nahum as "Matthew", Revelation 2:10 dropped entirely. A few are the preacher's own slips ("Acts 4:47", corrected to 2:47).
  5. The follow-up feature also causes some wrong verses. When the app has the wrong chapter open, "and verse 3" lands in that wrong chapter. Requiring a fresher open chapter would trade some of the 21 catches for fewer of these.
  6. Today's summary slide does not work for these preachers. It relies on phrases like "point number one", and not one of the 10 sermons used them. Half the slides read just "Sermon summary", and the points are sentence fragments.
  7. A small AI model running on the church's own computer writes a usable slide. It usually matches the reference's title and big idea and two or three of its points. It repeats verses across points and once invented a title. On this Mac it writes the slide in 20 to 35 seconds from the transcript so far, fast enough to refresh the summary every few minutes during the sermon.
  8. The speech model can fail silently. When this Mac was overloaded, one sermon lost 29 minutes of transcript with no warning. The app should show the operator when transcription stops.
